# First Three Days Narrative Rebuild

## Narrative promise

The opening should make ordinary arrival feel subtly unstable. The player is
not asked to solve the premise. They are asked to decide how much of themselves
to expose while the campus begins forming an opinion of them.

## Day 1 — First impressions

- Room 214 establishes Scott through behavior, objects, and conversational
  pressure rather than biography.
- The hallmates beat turns a mundane lunch invitation into the first belonging
  test. Reactions show the social consequence without explaining it.
- Lunch lets the player align with Doug, acknowledge Keith, or remain quiet.
- The evening choice converts those small social positions into a place the
  player chooses to be seen.

Design question: *Who do I let think they know me?*

## Day 2 — Recognition

- The room is now familiar enough to be named, but not comfortable.
- Scott's first line changes according to the player's Room 214 posture. The
  callback is one image or behavior—not a recap of the prior choice.
- The player can coordinate, stay noncommittal, or leave independently. Scott
  remembers coordination for later roommate content.
- Sensory details carry the temporal and psychological unease: old hardware,
  institutional textures, and a melody that will not resolve.

Design question: *Was yesterday a performance, or the beginning of a pattern?*

## Day 3 — Commitment

- Weekly planning is framed as a social act rather than a detached management
  screen.
- Similar social activities make different promises: an open dorm-room door is
  passive availability; cards in the lounge is a commitment to Doug's group.
- Activity copy avoids claiming affection for professors or routines the player
  has not experienced.

Design question: *What will my future absences mean to other people?*

## Acceptance criteria

- A warm, reserved, and practical playthrough all remain mechanically viable.
- Day 2 contains a visible but restrained callback to the Room 214 interaction.
- No reaction paragraph states a character's personality or the meaning of the
  player's choice when action or dialogue can carry it.
- The planner's first appearance has an in-world reason and no duplicate social
  proposition.
- Existing dates, Day 3 routine unlock, storylet keys, and database schema stay
  unchanged.
