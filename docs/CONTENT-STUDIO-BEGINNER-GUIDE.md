# Writing your first scenes in Content Studio

For someone new to narrative design. The longer [team guide](CONTENT-STUDIO-TEAM-GUIDE.md) covers review, releases and working with others; you do not need it to start.

## The ten-minute path

1. Open **Start here** (the first tab). It asks four things in order: what kind of scene, which storyline, when it appears, then the words.
2. **Write it.** A scene is a few lines the player reads, then two or three choices. Keep choice labels short and physical (“Pick up the phone”). Use “the man at the desk” instead of a name until the player could know it.
3. **Check and play it.** *Play this scene* lets you read it as a player would and shows what each choice does. *Checks* lists anything worth fixing, in plain words, with a one-click fix where one is safe.
4. **Save.** It goes into a private draft. Nothing changes for players until you publish.

You can come back to any scene from **Scenes** and edit it there. The *Script* tab is for writing; *Structured* holds flags, follow-ups and skill checks.

## Words you will meet

Hover any dotted word in Studio for its meaning, or open **Advanced → Glossary**. The short version:

| You will see | It means |
|---|---|
| Scene (also “storylet”, “moment”, “offer”) | One small moment the player can step into |
| Track | One of six storylines that run at once |
| Window | How many extra days a scene stays available. 0 means that day only, and a player who passes never sees it again |
| Flag | A note the game remembers so a later scene can react |
| Draft | Your private working copy |
| Release | A published version players use |

## Working alone

Normally a second person must review work before it goes live. If you are the only person on the team, an administrator can turn on **solo mode** in **My work → Working alone**. You can then approve and publish your own work. Every such release is permanently marked **self-reviewed**. Solo mode only works for a one-person team and switches itself off the moment a second member is added.

## Starting a fresh set of scenes

**My work → Starting a fresh set of scenes** can remove every scene from your draft and add eight small starter scenes (one for each of the six storylines, plus two follow-ups). The game needs at least one scene on each storyline to start a new run, so the starter scenes keep it bootable while you write real ones. They are original, unnamed-character examples of the patterns you will use: a flag set in one scene and read in another, a scene that needs an earlier one, a change to a storyline's state, and costs.

This only changes your draft. Players keep the version they are on until you publish, and people already mid-game stay on their current version.

## When something is not working

- **“Save failed … revision”:** someone else saved first. Your words are kept; compare, then save again.
- **A scene never appears in play:** check its day, part of day and window (the preview says why it is or is not offered), and that any flag or earlier scene it needs can actually happen.
- **The checks say “Worth fixing”:** these never block saving. “Must fix” items block publishing.
